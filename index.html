The perspective

Why move the camera to the pedestrian

Almost all crossing-intention prediction is exocentric. It informs the vehicle and presupposes an instrumented vehicle or roadside. Predicting from the pedestrian's own view changes not only the input but who the prediction is for.

It also means the accuracies below are comparative evidence under controlled conditions, not field performance.

Results

    
Four things the experiments showed

  


  
  

    

      01
      
Off-the-shelf models see the scene but do not know the traffic

      
With video alone, no zero-shot model reaches the task-specific CLIP+Transformer baseline, and several barely beat always answering "yield". Asking for chain-of-thought reasoning does not help and multiplies latency by ten. Marking the shuttle and the crossing goal in the frames (set-of-marks) does not help either. Low-rank fine-tuning removes the gap for both model families.

      02
      
The models read the clip, not a shortcut

      
A model could score well here while ignoring the video. So we corrupt the input and hold everything else fixed. For the video-only model, blank or mismatched frames drop accuracy to the majority-class rate, and reversing frame order hurts more than shuffling it: the model reads the direction of motion. For the gaze-guided model, corrupting frames costs little because the context stream carries the signal, and removing that stream at test time costs a lot.

      
Pick a corruption. Faded marks show the uncorrupted score.

    

    

      

      

      

    

  


  
  

    

      03
      
Gaze is unusable untrained and the best cue once learned

      
Prompted, gaze direction or on-screen fixations alone lower accuracy below the no-context input, and a single cue only works when ego motion gives it a frame of reference. After fine-tuning, ego motion plus gaze direction is the best model we have, at 0.835 accuracy on unseen participants. That is 14.9% above the baseline in relative terms and 7.7% above the same model fine-tuned on video alone.

      
Vehicle motion, the one cue vehicle-side sensing already provides, adds almost nothing in either regime.

    

    

      

    

  


  
  

    

      04
      
What adaptation costs depends on the run

      
We re-test every fine-tuned model on MMBench, a general visual-reasoning benchmark unrelated to traffic. Video-only fine-tuning loses a little, evenly. Gaze-guided fine-tuning with the same rank and epochs is a lottery: one seed keeps nearly all of its general ability, one loses some, one loses most. The mean hides this. The seed spread is the result.

Implications

    
What follows for practice

    

      

        
Gaze-capable eyewear, not a forward camera alone

        
Of every cue we tested, gaze yields the largest gain and vehicle motion the smallest. A device is worth what it senses.

      

      

        
A complement to vehicle-centric sensing

        
The model anticipates the wearer's own decision from what a head-mounted device already captures, with no instrumented vehicle or infrastructure, and it covers the occlusion cases vehicle sensors cannot.

      

      

        
Training stability is the deployment target

        
Gaze-guided adaptation can keep general capability or destroy it depending on the seed. Checkpoint selection on a general held-out set, or a rehearsal term, are the next things to try.

      

    

    

      
Read with these limits

      
    
        
  • VR data. Behaviour in VR has not been validated against real pedestrian–AV encounters, because no such recordings exist.
    • 
        
  • Uniform frame sampling. Brief but decisive cues can fall between frames.
    • 
        
  • On-device operation is a design constraint here, not a demonstrated result.
